Conservation Laws
for Diffusion Models

An information-theoretic connection between denoising and likelihood in discrete and continuous diffusion.

Ziv Aharoni & Henry D. PfisterDuke University

PaperCodeExperiments

Overview

A sentence can be corrupted by masking letters, replacing them at random, or adding noise to their embeddings. These processes remove information at different rates. If each ends with no information about the original sentence, the total information lost is the same.

We extend conservation laws from coding theory to diffusion models, building on Cyril Méasson’s Conservation Laws for Coding (2006). The paper shows how predictions about individual parts of the data, made at different noise levels, can be combined to characterize how well a model describes the full data distribution.

An illustration of three ways to erase information: hide parts of the data, replace them at random, or add Gaussian noise.

Choosing the noise process

The conservation law gives a common description of masked, uniform replacement, and Gaussian noise. For a fixed data distribution, the exact information total is independent of this choice. A trained model, however, may find one denoising task easier than another. We examine these differences on synthetic sequences, text, and images.

Technical details

For data distribution \(P_X\) and model distribution \(Q_X\), let \(\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathcal{T}^n\) be a path through the space of coordinate-wise noise parameters, where \(\mathcal{T}\) is the parameter space for a single coordinate. At path position \(\tau\), \(\gamma(\tau)=(\gamma_1(\tau),\ldots,\gamma_n(\tau))\) specifies the noise parameter for each variable. The path runs from clean data at \(\tau=0\) to pure noise at \(\tau=1\). Define the channel score \(S_t\) and the local contribution \(g_i\) as follows:

\[ \begin{aligned} S_t(x,y)&=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log W_t(y\mid x),\\[0.8em] g_i(\mathbf t) &=\mathbb E_P\!\left[ -S_{t_i}(X_i,Y_i)\log Q_{X_i\mid Y}(X_i\mid Y) +\mathbb E_{X'\sim Q_{X_i\mid Y}(\cdot\mid Y)} \!\left[S_{t_i}(X',Y_i)\mid Y\right] \right],\\[0.8em] \operatorname{CE}(P_X,Q_X) &=\int_0^1\sum_{i=1}^n\gamma_i^{\prime}(\tau)\, g_i\!\left(\gamma(\tau)\right)\,d\tau. \end{aligned} \]

Here, \(Y=Y(\mathbf t)\) is the noisy observation, \(W_t(y\mid x)\) is the forward noise channel, and \(S_t\) is its channel score. The outer expectations are taken under the data distribution and this channel.

The first term in \(g_i\) is the negative log-likelihood weighted by the channel score. The second is the posterior correction term; it averages to zero when the model matches the data distribution. The factor \(\gamma_i^{\prime}(\tau)\) accounts for how quickly the noise parameter of variable \(i\) changes along the path.

The conserved quantity is data–model cross-entropy. When the model matches the data distribution, it reduces to entropy. Its local contributions can be evaluated using marginal posteriors and the known noise channel.

The exact identity assumes an admissible (smooth) memoryless channel family, posteriors factored with the true channel, and mild regularity conditions, with the clean and pure-noise endpoints specified above.
